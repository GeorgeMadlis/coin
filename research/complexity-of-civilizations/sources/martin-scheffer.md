### From the source transcript

The study applied survival analysis to 324 pre-modern states over 4,000 years of history. It found:

- States have a median lifespan of roughly **200 years**
- States become **more vulnerable to collapse as they age** — not because of any single cause, but because accumulated complexity creates brittleness
- The vulnerability is intrinsic to the aging process itself; there is no identifiable single "cause" of collapse in most cases
- The pattern holds across regions and historical periods, suggesting a structural regularity rather than contingent historical factors

### Methodological note (from secondary descriptions)

Survival analysis is a statistical technique from biomedical research used to model time-to-event data. Applying it to states treats "collapse" as the event and historical duration as the exposure variable, allowing the researchers to estimate baseline hazard rates and test whether age itself is a risk factor independent of other covariates. This is a rigorous quantitative approach to a question previously addressed mainly through case-study narrative.

### Relation to other sources

Scheffer's finding that complexity accumulates into brittleness over time provides empirical backing for Tainter's theoretical model of diminishing marginal returns. The 200-year median lifespan, replicated across 324 states, gives Tainter's qualitative theory a quantitative baseline.
