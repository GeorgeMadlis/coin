## *The Essays or Counsels, Civil and Moral* (1597/1625, Project Gutenberg)

**Access status:** open (full text)  
**URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/575

The collection comprises 58 essays. It grew from 10 essays in 1597 through successive editions, reaching its final form in 1625, one year before Bacon's death. Project Gutenberg eBook #575 provides the complete text in EPUB, Kindle, HTML, and plain text formats.

### The essay relevant to the source transcript: "Of Seditions and Troubles"

The claim attributed to Bacon in the source transcript: *"Sedition follows when more are bred scholars than preferment can take off"* — cited by Peter Turchin as a 400-year-old anticipation of his elite overproduction theory.

"Of Seditions and Troubles" examines the causes of civil unrest and how rulers may prevent or manage it. Bacon identifies two general causes of sedition: **material** (poverty, discontent) and **structural** (an oversupply of educated men relative to the positions available to absorb them). The second cause is the specific anticipation: a society that produces more ambitious, educated aspirants than it can employ or honour generates a surplus that becomes politically dangerous.

### Core argument of the *Essays* as a whole

The Essays apply Bacon's empirical-inductive method to political and moral life. They examine topics from public affairs to private conduct — truth, death, friendship, ambition, studies, unity — using systematic argument that weighs competing perspectives rather than asserting doctrine. Bacon treats political wisdom as a subject for careful, evidence-based analysis in the same spirit he applied to natural philosophy.

### Key claims relevant to the source transcript's context

1. **Elite overproduction precedes sedition:** Surplus educated men who cannot find preferment become a structural source of political instability — a claim Turchin validated quantitatively four centuries later.
2. **Structural causes, not moral failings:** Bacon frames sedition as a predictable structural outcome of social conditions, not simply the result of bad actors — anticipating the systems-level analysis common to all the authors in this research.
3. **Preventive statecraft:** Rulers can reduce sedition risk by controlling the output of educational institutions and ensuring sufficient opportunities exist for the ambitious — implying that the problem is manageable if recognised early.

### Methodology

Political philosophy and moral essay. Bacon draws on classical history, contemporary political observation, and systematic reasoning. Unlike his natural philosophy, the Essays do not use controlled experiment but apply the same empirical-observational habit of mind to human affairs.
