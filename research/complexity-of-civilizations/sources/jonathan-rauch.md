# Jonathan Rauch — Sources

---

## *The Constitution of Knowledge: A Defense of Truth* (2021, Brookings Institution Press)

**Access status:** open (publisher page)  
**URL:** https://www.brookings.edu/books/the-constitution-of-knowledge/

### Core argument

Liberal democracy and science share a common institutional foundation — what Rauch calls the **"Constitution of Knowledge"** — a social system for converting disagreement into truth through adversarial testing, revision, and consensus. This system did not emerge spontaneously; it was built over centuries and depends on institutional maintenance. It is currently under coordinated attack from disinformation, social media pile-ons, conspiracy theories, and what Rauch terms "epistemic cancel culture."

The book's central contention: the crisis of the 2010s–2020s is not primarily a crisis of politics or economics but of epistemology — a breakdown in shared standards for establishing what is true.

### Key claims

1. **The Constitution of Knowledge is a social technology:** Like political constitutions, it comprises rules, institutions, and practices for managing disagreement productively rather than violently. It is not simply "science" but the entire ecosystem of fact-checking, peer review, adversarial journalism, and open debate.
2. **Historical parallel:** The epistemic constitution emerged in parallel with liberal democracy in the eighteenth century — the same period, the same intellectual roots. Their fates are intertwined.
3. **Contemporary threats are qualitatively new:** Russian disinformation campaigns and social media dynamics exploit the openness of liberal epistemology to flood it with noise, making the signal-to-noise problem structural rather than incidental.
4. **"Reality-based community" defence:** Rauch argues citizens can actively defend objective truth — epistemic institutions are not simply victims but can develop resilience strategies.
5. **Fragility is structural:** The knowledge constitution, like political constitutions, is not self-enforcing; it requires active defence by participants who understand how it works.

### Methodology

Intellectual history and political philosophy, combined with contemporary political analysis. Rauch draws on the history of science (Popper, Kuhn), liberal political theory (Mill, Locke), and current events to construct a normative and descriptive account of how truth is produced and defended.

### Relevance to complexity-of-civilizations research

Rauch's primary relevance in the source transcript is his characterisation of Mancur Olson's *The Logic of Collective Action* as having "blew a hole in the hull of American political science." The *Constitution of Knowledge* is not directly about civilisational collapse, but its analysis of epistemic institution decay is a concrete instance of the Olsonian problem: the institutions that produce reliable knowledge are themselves subject to capture by distributional coalitions and bad-faith actors.
