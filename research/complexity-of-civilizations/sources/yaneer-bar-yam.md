## 1. "The Food Crises and Political Instability in North Africa and the Middle East" (2011)

**Access status:** open  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.2455  
**Authors:** Marco Lagi, Karla Z. Bertrand, Yaneer Bar-Yam  
**Published:** August 2011, New England Complex Systems Institute (NECSI)

### Core argument

Global food price volatility functions as a critical trigger for political instability, not merely a background condition. The paper demonstrates a quantifiable relationship between FAO food price index levels and the timing of violent protest. Above a specific threshold, instability becomes statistically probable regardless of other political conditions. An underlying upward trend in food prices (independent of spikes) threatened to cross into "high impacts" territory by 2012–2013 even without temporary volatility.

The analysis was submitted to the US government on 13 December 2010. The Arab Spring began four days later, on 17 December 2010.

### Key claims

1. **Price-protest correlation:** Large global food price peaks align temporally with violent protests in 2011 and riots in 2008, indicating a causal rather than coincidental relationship.
2. **Quantifiable threshold:** A specific FAO food price index level (approximately 210) exists above which social unrest becomes statistically likely — a hard number submitted as a policy warning before events occurred.
3. **Trend threat independent of spikes:** Even without price volatility, the underlying upward pricing trend would drive the index into dangerous territory by 2012–2013.
4. **Compounding effects:** Food crises compound pre-existing political failures and unemployment, but price itself is the proximate trigger for mass action.
5. **Urgency imperative:** Preventing future disruption requires rapid and concerted action on global food systems — the mechanisms are now well-understood enough to act on.

### Methodology

Quantitative analysis of FAO global food price data. Statistical correlation between pricing events and documented protest incidents. Trend extrapolation to identify future risk windows. Physics-based approach to socioeconomic phenomena (NECSI specialisation). Temporal analysis to test prediction precision.

## 2. "Global Pattern Formation and Ethnic/Cultural Violence" (2007, *Science*)

**Access status:** paywalled  
**URL:** https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1142734  
**Authors:** M. Lim, R. Metzler, Y. Bar-Yam  
**Published:** *Science* 317(5844), 14 September 2007  
[Full text not retrieved — abstract only]

### From published descriptions and the source transcript

The paper derives a mathematical model showing that geographic ethnic patch size predicts inter-group violence with 90% geographic correlation. Ethnic patches between 20 and 60 km wide are most associated with violent conflict; patches smaller or larger than this range show significantly lower rates of violence. The model is derived from complex-systems pattern-formation theory.

The implication is that **borders matter geometrically**, not just politically — the spatial structure of ethnic intermixing is a primary driver of violence, independent of the specific groups or political history involved.

## 3. *Dynamics of Complex Systems* (1997, Addison-Wesley / Westview Press)

**Access status:** open (full text freely available)  
**URL:** https://necsi.edu/dynamics-of-complex-systems  
**Author:** Yaneer Bar-Yam  
**Publisher:** Westview Press ("Studies in Nonlinearity" series)  
**ISBN:** 0813341213

### Core argument

Complex systems constitute a unified scientific discipline. Fundamental principles governing how complexity emerges from simple components apply across radically different domains — neural networks, protein folding, evolution, and human civilisation. The book is the first unified textbook for this discipline.

### Key topics covered

- Iterative maps and chaos theory
- Probability and random walks
- Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics
- Information and computation theory
- Fractals and scaling
- Neural networks and cognition
- Protein folding dynamics
- Evolution and development
- Human civilisation and complexity transitions

### Methodology

Mathematical and theoretical physics approach to interdisciplinary complex systems science. Combines formal mathematical treatment with computational modelling. Author recommends starting with chapters 2, 3, 8, and 9.
