# Sources and Works - Evidence First, Consensus Later

**Inquiry:** "Earth-related digital twins cannot automatically resolve conflicts over environmental governance, but they can make those conflicts more inspectable by linking claims to data, models, assumptions, uncertainty, provenance, and governance decisions."

---

## Core programmes and concepts

**European Commission - Destination Earth.** Relevant as the major European policy programme framing high-resolution Earth-system digital twins as environmental decision-support infrastructure.

**ConnectedNature / COIN - Evidence bundle design.** The prior ConnectedNature inquiry, "Evidence First, Currency Later," supplies the evidence-bundle principle used here: downstream instruments should reference an inspectable evidence layer rather than substitute for it.

**Blockchain provenance literature.** Relevant for the distinction between record integrity and scientific truth. Blockchain anchoring can preserve hashes, timestamps, versions, and review events, but cannot validate ecological models or settle value disputes.

**Agentic AI / retrieval-augmented analysis.** Relevant for the bounded role of AI systems as source finders, claim extractors, inconsistency detectors, and summarizers of disagreement.

## Estonian forest-management sources

**National Geographic - "Estonia's 'holy forests' threatened by demand for biofuels."**
Reports that Estonia had one of the highest rates of forest loss in Europe, citing a Nature paper. Used to support the forest-loss-rate wording.
URL: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/estonia-holy-forests-threatened-by-industrial-tree-farming

**BirdLife Europe - "Of Clearcuts and Birds #3 - Estonia."**
Reports sharp increases in harvested forest areas in 2016-2018 compared with 2004-2015 and states that logging is usually done by clear-cutting. Used to support clear-cutting prevalence with qualification.
URL: https://www.birdlife.org/news/2022/09/05/of-clearcuts-and-birds-part3-estonia/

**PLOS ONE - "Modeling forest landscape futures: Full scale simulation of ..."**
Describes Estonian forest management as almost entirely clear-cutting based. Used to support the "predominantly clear-cutting based" sentence.
URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0294650

**Eurostat - "Forest growth in the EU outpaces harvesting" (2026).**
Reports that in 2023 Estonia was the only EU country with available data where wood removals exceeded net annual increment. Used as a harvesting-pressure source, not as direct evidence of clear-cutting rankings.
URL: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/edn-20260320-2

**Statistics Estonia - "Forest."**
Lists 2023 forestry indicators including removals of 11,564 thousand cubic metres and net increment of 9,100 thousand cubic metres. Used to corroborate the Eurostat quantities in national statistical form.
URL: https://stat.ee/en/find-statistics/statistics-theme/environment/forest

**IEA Bioenergy - "Implementation of bioenergy in Estonia" country report (2021).**
Reports that in 2007-2017 the average felling volume was 8.8 million cubic metres per year and average annual increment of growing stock in managed forests was 12.8 million cubic metres per year. Used as one of the alternative wood-balance benchmark definitions.
URL: https://www.ieabioenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/CountryReport2021_Estonia_final.pdf

**Estonian Climate Ministry - "Forest management and bioenergy."**
States that in 2011-2019 average felling was 10.7 million cubic metres and average annual increment was 16.2 million cubic metres. Used as the higher national increment benchmark.
URL: https://kliimaministeerium.ee/forest-management-and-bioenergy

**Sustainable Biomass Program - Revised Regional Risk Assessment for Estonia (2024).**
Summarises carbon-stock projections in which business-as-usual felling around 11.5 million cubic metres leads to lower 2050 growing stock, while additional measures with 9.4-9.8 million cubic metres maintain or increase growing stock. Used for the carbon-stability verdict.
URL: https://sbp-cert.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/SBP_RRA_Estonia_consultation_final.pdf

**European Commission - Climate Action Progress Report 2025, Estonia factsheet.**
Reports Estonia's LULUCF net emissions and accounted debit under current rules. Used to distinguish wood-balance sufficiency from LULUCF/carbon-accounting performance.
URL: https://climate.ec.europa.eu/document/download/7e8c0b6e-ad7b-40ac-97e8-17e38bd764f9_en?filename=ee_2025_factsheet_en.pdf

## Climate attribution and false-equivalence boundary

**IPCC assessment literature.** Relevant for the article's claim that anthropogenic climate-change attribution is not an open dispute in the same way as many land-use or forest-management conflicts.

**Climate attribution literature.** Relevant for distinguishing scientifically open questions, such as feedback magnitudes and regional impacts, from politically amplified controversy around settled attribution basics.
