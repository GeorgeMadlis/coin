# Authors and Works — Ecological Limits in Transactions

**Inquiry:** "Ecological limits will remain politically weak and economically secondary until they are built into transactions themselves rather than handled afterward by taxes, standards, and reporting."

---

## Primary source authors

**Merit Valdsalu** — Co-author, Single.Earth Foundation Working Paper No. 1 (2026).
Contact: merit@single.earth. Associated with the Single.Earth initiative, which works on
nature-backed markets and ecosystem accounting. Contribution: conceptual framework for
regeneration-linked unit issuance and transaction-level burn mechanism.

**Andrus Aaslaid** — Co-author, Single.Earth Foundation Working Paper No. 1 (2026).
Associated with Single.Earth. Contribution: mechanism design, institutional design analysis,
and governance discussion.

## Key works cited in the source paper

**Herman Daly** — *Beyond Growth: The Economics of Sustainable Development* (1996).
Beacon Press. The foundational text for the steady-state economics argument: that economic
throughput cannot be indefinitely expanded within a finite biosphere, and that price signals
alone cannot govern material flows. Provides the theoretical grounding for the ex ante vs.
ex post distinction.

**Partha Dasgupta** — *The Economics of Biodiversity: The Dasgupta Review* (2021).
HM Treasury. The comprehensive review of how natural capital is systematically underpriced
in national accounts and market systems. Relevant to the claim that ecological limits are
economically secondary: Dasgupta explains why this is structurally the case and what kinds
of accounting reforms are needed.

**Johan Rockström et al.** — "A safe operating space for humanity," *Nature* 461, 472–475
(2009). The planetary boundaries framework. Establishes quantitative estimates for nine
Earth-system boundaries within which human societies can operate safely. The framework
provides the biophysical basis for the claim that current economic activity exceeds
regenerative capacity.

**United Nations** — *System of Environmental-Economic Accounting — Ecosystem Accounting*
(SEEA-EA, 2021). The international standard for ecosystem accounting, providing the
framework for translating ecological conditions into units compatible with national
economic statistics. The proposed mechanism draws on SEEA for its measurement foundations.

**Dias et al.** — "Using essential biodiversity variables to assess forest ecosystem
integrity," *Frontiers in Forests and Global Change* 6:1098901 (2023). The Ecosystem
Integrity Index. Cited in the paper as a potential measurement framework for tracking
ecological condition at higher resolution than conventional accounts.

## Works not cited but relevant for further investigation

**Robert Costanza et al.** — "The value of the world's ecosystem services and natural
capital," *Nature* 387, 253–260 (1997). Early attempt to monetize ecosystem services;
relevant background for measurement challenges.

**Grischa Perino et al.** — "Understanding Cancellation in the EU ETS," *Nature Climate
Change* 9, 408–410 (2019). On the Market Stability Reserve and its effects on EU ETS
price dynamics; directly relevant to evaluating the ex post instrument record.

**Ottmar Edenhofer et al.** (IPCC WG3) — IPCC AR6 Working Group III (2022). Chapter 13
on national and sub-national policies provides the most comprehensive current assessment
of carbon pricing effectiveness across jurisdictions.

**Kate Raworth** — *Doughnut Economics* (2017). A popular synthesis of the ecological
limits argument with distributional considerations; useful context for the distributional
effects section.
