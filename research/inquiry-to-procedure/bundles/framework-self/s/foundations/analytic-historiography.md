## Summary

Danto 1965 emphasizes narrative sentences that describe past events in light of later developments. Ankersmit 1983 treats historical narratives as organizing structures rather than simple copies of the past. These ideas show that historical meaning depends on later description and narrative form. They are relevant when observers dispute thinly recorded events.

## What it explains

It explains why historical interpretation is retrospective and why narratives can organize the same traces differently.

## What it fails to explain

It does not give a computational formulation of observer bounds or a clear mechanism for declaring irrecoverable facts.

## What this framework adds

This framework adds the computational-boundedness dimension for narrative compression and an explicit recording function for the surviving record. It extends analytic historiography by classifying when disagreement is frame-level and when it is factual irreducibility.

## Classification

Addresses Type II and Type III in both static and sequential regimes.
