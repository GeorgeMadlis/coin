## Summary

Simon 1955 argues that human decision-makers do not optimize with unlimited information and computation. They satisfice under cognitive, informational, and environmental limits. This gives a standard vocabulary for why observers simplify. It supports the idea that disagreement can come from bounded processing rather than bad faith.

## What it explains

It explains why observers use partial searches, heuristics, and good-enough summaries under limits.

## What it fails to explain

It does not give a formal account of why two bounded observers form different frames from the same surviving record.

## What this framework adds

This framework extends Simon by specifying the computational-boundedness dimension as compression over a surviving record. It also adds an explicit recording function that distinguishes present cognitive limits from past losses in the record.

## Classification

Addresses Type I and Type II in both static and sequential regimes.
