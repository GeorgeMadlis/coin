## Summary

Peirce 1877 describes inquiry as movement from doubt toward the fixation of belief. The emphasis is procedural: inquiry unfolds because doubt disrupts settled belief. This is close to the framework's idea of rounds driven by questions, objections, and evidence. It also supports treating single-party self-revision as a genuine inquiry process.

## What it explains

It explains why inquiry is temporal and why a later belief can supersede an earlier one.

## What it fails to explain

It does not provide a computational account of bounded compression or a detailed file-level recording discipline.

## What this framework adds

This framework adds the computational-boundedness dimension to Peirce's temporal model. It also adds an explicit recording function that records each move and revision so later observers can re-enter the statement trajectory.

## Classification

Addresses Type I and Type II in the sequential regime.
