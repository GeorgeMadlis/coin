Summary

Duhem 1906 and Quine 1951 argue that evidence tests networks of assumptions rather than isolated claims. When observation conflicts with theory, several parts of the web may be adjusted. This helps explain why evidence can fail to decide between interpretations. It is a central anchor for underdetermination.

What it explains

It explains why the same evidence can support multiple theory-plus-auxiliary packages.

What it fails to explain

It does not specify why the surviving record is insufficient or how record loss differs from frame disagreement.

What this framework adds

This framework adds an explicit recording function that identifies when disputed content lies outside R. It also adds the computational-boundedness dimension, separating hard frame translation from factual absence.

Classification

Addresses Type II and Type III in the static regime.
