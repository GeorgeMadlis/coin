## Summary

Bayesian updating describes how probabilities change by conditioning on evidence. Alchourron, Gardenfors, and Makinson 1985 give postulates for belief revision and contraction when beliefs face new information. Both traditions model rational change over time. They provide a natural anchor for statement trajectories as sequences of updates.

## What it explains

It explains how an observer can move from one belief state to another after receiving evidence or facing inconsistency.

## What it fails to explain

It often assumes strong idealization such as logical omniscience, and it does not focus on lossy recording of the inquiry itself.

## What this framework adds

This framework adds the computational-boundedness dimension by treating update and translation as costly for limited observers. It also adds an explicit recording function, so the sequence of revisions is preserved rather than reconstructed from memory.

## Classification

Addresses Type I and Type II in the sequential regime.
