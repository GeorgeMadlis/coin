Summary

Aumann 1976 shows that ideal Bayesian agents with common priors cannot have common knowledge of posterior disagreement. If they share the same prior and know each other's posterior beliefs, persistent agreement to disagree is ruled out. The result is useful because it makes disagreement informative: something about priors, partitions, or knowledge conditions must differ. It is a clean static benchmark rather than a model of real inquiry.

What it explains

It explains why persistent disagreement implies a structural difference between observers when ideal Bayesian conditions are assumed.

What it fails to explain

It does not model bounded agents, lossy records, sequential inquiry, or frame translation costs.

What this framework adds

This framework adds the computational-boundedness dimension by treating observers as limited compressors rather than ideal Bayesians. It also adds an explicit recording function, so disagreement can be traced to the surviving record as well as to priors or partitions.

Classification

Addresses Type I and Type II in the static regime.

References

  • Robert J. Aumann, "Agreeing to Disagree," The Annals of Statistics 4(6), 1976. DOI: 10.1214/aos/1176343654.
