Limitations And Falsifiability

This document fills the Step 8 deliverable. It is intentionally adversarial. The framework is useful only if it can name the conditions under which it is redundant, circular, unmeasurable, non-generalizable, or wrong.

The most important current limitation is empirical: the only populated case study is bundles/framework-self/, a self-referential trajectory generated by the same framework and agents later used to assess it. The sample size is one. The four external case-study bundles remain stubs. Nothing in this repository yet validates population-level claims about legal, scientific, historical, institutional, or public disputes.

1. Theoretical Limits

Wolfram observer theory is used here as a formal analogy, not as established physics for social, epistemic, legal, or institutional systems. The framework does not show that human or AI disagreement literally inhabits rulial space. It borrows a computational vocabulary: bounded observers, coarse-graining, compression, reducibility, and trajectory.

That vocabulary may be more redescriptive than explanatory. If "compression function" only renames prior, method, legal rule, ideology, disciplinary training, or narrative frame without improving prediction, diagnosis, or record design, then the framework adds little beyond metaphor.

Existing theories may already explain much of the target space:

  • Aumann-style Bayesian disagreement, partitions, common priors, and common knowledge already explain why agreement is constrained under strong rationality assumptions.
  • Bounded rationality, belief revision, argumentation theory, dialogue games, second-order cybernetics, social epistemology, archival theory, and provenance/reproducibility research already cover many parts of the model.
  • Quine-Duhem underdetermination and analytic historiography already explain why lost historical facts cannot be recovered from surviving traces alone.

The framework's candidate contribution is the combination of an explicit recording function, a contestation-type taxonomy, a five-stage trajectory vocabulary, and an operational bundle discipline. If later work shows that this combination does not produce better classifications, fewer repeated disputes, or clearer underdetermination decisions than existing approaches, the framework should be reformulated as a documentation profile rather than a theory.

The observer model also oversimplifies disagreement. Social and institutional disputes are not only epistemic-computational events. They can be driven by interest, identity, fear, loyalty, emotion, attention markets, law, coercion, reputation, strategic ambiguity, and power. Compressing all of these into f_i may hide the very causal variables that matter. The computational account is most plausible when parties are trying to answer a stable question from records and methods. It is weakest when parties benefit from non-resolution or when the dispute is about values, authority, entitlement, or enforcement.

2. Formal Limits

The static model E -> r -> R -> O_i -> y_i is underdetermined in practice. For any observed output, many triples (r, C, f) can explain it. A party's answer can be modeled as missing context, a different frame, a different recording process, strategic behavior, or several of these at once. This non-uniqueness is not a minor inconvenience; it limits causal diagnosis.

Frames are difficult to identify from outputs. Two observers can emit the same answer for different reasons, or different answers for the same underlying reason expressed in different vocabularies. Without independent records of decision rules, prompts, priors, thresholds, codebooks, and rejected alternatives, f is inferred after the fact.

Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable. Description length and compressed bytes are only proxies. They depend on representation, language, tokenization, compressor, prompt, audience, and allowed notation. A compression metric may measure style, fluency, or the evaluator's preferred representation rather than frame distance.

The Type I/II/III taxonomy can overlap:

  • A Type I evidence gap can expose a Type II frame gap after sharing.
  • An apparent Type II frame gap can actually be Type III missing provenance.
  • A Type III diagnosis can be premature when the relevant record exists but has not yet been found, parsed, licensed, or afforded.
  • A mixed claim may contain Type I, Type II, and Type III subclaims at once.

Type III is especially fragile. Strictly, it is a claim about non-identifiability from R: compatible event streams differ on the target proposition. In real records, this may be unknowable rather than positively identifiable. A later archive can appear, changing R; a better inference rule can show that an aggregate was sufficient after all; or a missingness judgment can reflect the observer's limited search. Therefore Type III closures should be provisional unless the record boundary and the target proposition are tightly specified.

P4 type descent can fail under frame oscillation, adversarial moves, strategic ambiguity, evidence deletion, evidence flooding, unbounded frame invention, mixed subclaims, and non-monotonic belief revision. Truthfulness and fairness assumptions are load-bearing. If they do not hold, descent is at best a coding aspiration, not a process law.

P4' stage ordering is weaker still. Formation precedes later stages by definition, but enrichment, critique, contestation, and consolidation are not linearly ordered. Consolidation is provisional and supersedable; it is not a terminal sink. A trajectory can consolidate, reopen, enrich, critique, and contest again. Stage-level "movement toward consolidation" may not generalize.

P5 dialogic irreducibility may be too broad. If every rich inquiry is declared irreducible, the claim may fail to distinguish genuine unpredictability from poor modeling, insufficient features, or deliberate refusal to model routine procedures. A bounded version of P5 is needed: which classes of trajectories are predictably shortcuttable, which are not, and by what evidence?

3. Stage Taxonomy Stress Test

The five stages are useful only if they survive adversarial classification. They are not guaranteed to be exhaustive, mutually exclusive, stable across observers, or identifiable from records.

Exhaustiveness risks:

  • Administrative maintenance may update a bundle without clearly forming, enriching, critiquing, contesting, or consolidating a claim.
  • Translation, summarization, and migration moves may preserve meaning while altering representation.
  • Enforcement, sanction, or implementation moves may apply a settled claim rather than evolve it.

Mutual exclusivity risks:

  • A round can add evidence while also advocating a rival position.
  • A critique can be so strong that it functions as contestation.
  • A consolidation can hide unresolved disagreement by adopting a convention under authority pressure.
  • A verdict revision can both consolidate a new answer and contest the prior answer.

Observer stability risks:

  • One coder may treat "I worry this assumption is wrong" as critique; another may treat it as contestation if it implies a rival position.
  • One party may see a new source as enrichment; another may see it as evidence flooding.
  • A party with institutional power may label a settlement as consolidation while weaker parties experience it as unresolved contestation.

Record-identifiability risks:

  • A transcript may omit tone, negotiation, coercion, private commitments, or off-record coordination.
  • A round file may state only the official move, not the strategic purpose.
  • Retrospective stage labels may be inferred from outcomes, creating circular evidence for stage sequence claims.

Ambiguous examples:

example plausible classification adversarial reading
"Adding three new citations to support the current method." Enrichment. Covert advocacy if the citations are selected to overwhelm a pending objection.
"Can you justify this threshold?" Critique. Contestation if the questioner is maintaining an alternative threshold.
"We adopt the regulator's rule for now." Consolidation. Power-driven convention hiding live disagreement.
"The result changes because we corrected the dataset version." Enrichment or consolidation. Contestation against the old result if the prior verdict remains live.
"No further evidence is recoverable." Consolidation as underdetermined. Premature closure if search costs, privacy limits, or access barriers are being mistaken for absence from R.

These examples imply a design requirement: every stage label should carry a rationale and, where appropriate, uncertainty. Mixed moves should either be split into smaller rounds or marked as mixed with a primary stage and a secondary-stage note. Stage labels alone must not be treated as evidence that the stage taxonomy is valid.

4. Recording And Provenance Limits

The recording function is not epistemically neutral. Archives are shaped by power, incentives, cost, technical affordances, legal obligations, institutional memory, publication bias, deletion practices, and historical selection. Treating r as a clean map risks laundering those choices into "the record."

Provenance completeness does not guarantee truth. It can show that a source, version, checksum, environment, or chain of custody is recorded. It cannot show that the source was honest, the instrument valid, the witness unbiased, the schema appropriate, the interpretation correct, or the omitted material irrelevant.

Exhaustive recording can create noise and evidence flooding. More material may make a later observer less able to identify the decisive records. A bad-faith party can exploit the bundle discipline by adding many low-quality records that are technically preserved but practically obstructive.

Append-only records preserve error as well as correction. Supersession makes old mistakes recoverable, which is useful for provenance, but it can also let stale claims be quoted out of context. Readers must distinguish current concept state from historical round content.

Canonical Git history depends on repository governance. The rule that main is canonical works only if maintainers prevent force-pushes, unauthorized edits, and governance capture. Git integrity is not institutional legitimacy.

Derived artifacts can become stale. Rendered HTML, catalogs, exported zips, and snapshots are only trustworthy when pinned to a source commit and clearly marked as derived. A stale artifact can mislead even if it was generated correctly.

Hashes establish integrity, not accuracy. A checksum can prove that a file is unchanged. It cannot prove that the file is true, representative, complete, ethically usable, or relevant to the claim.

5. Behavioral And Adversarial Limits

The framework assumes, or at least benefits from, sincere inquiry. It degrades when parties use the record strategically.

behavior possible retrospective signals what may remain indistinguishable
Motivated reasoning Selective citation, asymmetric standards, repeated rejection of contrary evidence, stable conclusion despite changing rationale. Deeply held sincere priors can look identical.
Strategic ambiguity Refusal to define terms, shifting scope, incompatible uses of the same label. Genuine uncertainty while a concept is forming.
Bad-faith objections Repeated objections already answered, no update after answers, objections timed to delay closure. Persistent but sincere concern about the answer.
Evidence flooding Many low-relevance records, duplicated sources, rising evidence count without targeted state change. Legitimate broad literature review or archival dump.
Frame churn Frequent frame changes, moving thresholds, no stable comparison relation. Real discovery that the prior frame was inadequate.
Agent hallucination Unsupported citations, invented file states, contradictions with repository records. Honest summarization error unless source checking is logged.
Agent-specific priors Systematic party differences in labels, stages, or confidence. Domain expertise or better calibration by one agent.
Model updates between rounds Sudden style, capability, or policy changes correlated with model version changes. Learning from newly recorded material.
Label manipulation Parties argue about stage/type labels to gain procedural advantage. Legitimate methodological disagreement about classification.
Bookkeeping-as-quality Complete metadata but weak evidence, overconfidence from clean ledgers. Good process paired with genuine epistemic improvement.

The record can make some patterns retrospectively visible, especially repeated scope shifts, label changes, citation selection, and unresolved objections. It cannot reliably infer intent. Many adversarial behaviors remain observationally equivalent to sincere inquiry unless there are independent signals such as private communications, incentives, prior commitments, or external audits.

6. Empirical Limits

The framework-self pilot is self-referential. The framework helped generate the bundle, the stage labels, the metrics, the predictions, and the evidence later used to assess the framework. This creates circularity risk: the pilot may show only that the repository can be made to conform to its own categories.

Specific empirical limits:

  • The sample size is one trajectory.
  • Stages for early rounds were retrospectively inferred.
  • Codex and Claude are not independent random observers; they are related AI systems operating under similar task prompts and repository constraints.
  • Repository development is not representative of legal disputes, scientific controversies, historical reconstruction, public argument, or institutional adjudication.
  • The pilot provides no causal claim. It cannot show that bundle discipline caused lower re-litigation, better convergence, or better provenance.
  • The original four external case studies remain unexecuted: bundles/historical-type-iii/, bundles/climate-repro-pilot/, bundles/eudr-estonia-aoi/, and bundles/self-inquiry-eudr-docs/.
  • Because the framework itself shaped the evidence record, successful internal consistency is weak evidence for external validity.

The current evidence can support only modest claims: the repository can record rounds, preserve supersession, compute some metadata-derived metrics, and expose missing values. It cannot yet establish that the taxonomy is reliable, that the predictions hold, or that the framework improves real-world dispute handling.

7. Operational Limits

Full round discipline is expensive. It requires writing round records, frontmatter, rationales, provenance, supersession links, ledgers, validation, and sometimes derived artifacts. This can slow inquiry, discourage participation, or shift effort from substance to bookkeeping.

Maintenance burden accumulates. As bundles grow, links can rot, derived artifacts can become stale, conventions can drift, namespaces can collide, and schemas can migrate. Version migration needs explicit rounds; silent migration would undermine the record.

The profile may be hard for nontechnical participants. Git, Markdown frontmatter, hashes, commits, and validators are not neutral access mechanisms. They privilege technically fluent contributors unless supported by interfaces, training, or lightweight profiles.

Privacy and sensitive evidence create tension with append-only provenance. Immutable records can conflict with correction rights, deletion rights, confidentiality, safety, data minimization, and legal discovery risk. Some records should be summarized, redacted, encrypted, access-controlled, or not recorded at all. That weakens recoverability but may be ethically required.

OKF is draft-status infrastructure. OKF v0.1 may change; future versions may reserve keys that collide with local conventions. The fc- namespace mitigates but does not eliminate future namespace collisions.

Lightweight profiles trade rigor for usability. They may be appropriate for low-stakes, early, private, or exploratory work, but they reduce the ability to diagnose Type III missingness, reproduce metrics, and audit stage/type labels. The framework must specify when lightweight discipline is acceptable rather than pretending that every inquiry can afford the full profile.

8. Falsification Registry

P1: Type II convergence is practically blocked by frame-translation cost. Compare Type II disputes with measured frame-distance/translation cost against closure time after data equalization. Larger frame distance predicts longer persistence; closure follows translation, adopted frame, or convention. Data-only rounds often close Type II cases, or frame-distance measures are unstable. Rich, independently coded Type II disputes routinely converge cheaply without frame work. Pre/post outputs, shared records, frame codebooks, translation attempts, closure outcomes. Residual remains underdetermined; only convention or new R changes status. Later analysis shows retained records were sufficient. A correctly diagnosed Type III fact is recovered using only original R. Frozen record boundary, target proposition, intervention log, independent audit of retained sufficiency. Revise Type III definition or the non-invertibility argument. Derivable in formal model; empirically untested here.
P3: Lost recorder specification causes Type III to be misread as Type II. Study persistent disputes with missing r specification and code initial diagnoses before missingness audit. Missing recorder specs correlate with Type II labels later revised to Type III/underdetermined. Observers often mark underdetermination immediately despite missing specs. Observers reliably detect Type III without recorder specs, or misclassification is mostly Type I/bad-faith rather than Type II. Historical/provenance cases, initial labels, missingness audits, later classification changes. Narrow P3 to specific domains or remove "systematically." Conjecture; no external cases executed.
P4: Truthful recorded contestation descends I -> II -> III and bottoms out. Independently code multi-round contestation stretches under truthfulness/fairness inclusion rules. Type I components close before Type II, Type II before Type III residues; stalls trigger justified reclassification. Frequent stalls/reversals without clear mixed-claim or adversarial explanation. Well-recorded truthful stretches regularly resolve Type II/III before accessible Type I or oscillate without explanation. Multiple contestation stretches, blinded type coding, evidence/frame move log, truthfulness/adversarial indicators. Recast descent as optional diagnostic pattern, not proposition. Open; framework-self has only one contestation round.
P4': Formation precedes later stages; trajectories may trend toward consolidation. Code many stage trajectories prospectively and measure transition/consolidation/supersession patterns. Formation is first by definition; critique before contestation predicts shorter contestation and lower supersession. Stage sequences cycle heavily; critique has no association with later stability. Prospectively coded trajectories show no consolidation tendency and pre-contestation critique predicts equal or worse outcomes. Stage-labeled trajectories, consolidation rules, supersession follow-up, task controls. Keep P4'(a) only; drop or narrow P4'(b). P4'(a) definitional; P4'(b) unvalidated.
P5: Genuine trajectories cannot generally be predicted without running rounds. Ask predictors to forecast final claims, stages, type paths, and status from early prefixes across rich trajectories. Forecast accuracy is poor for nontrivial evidence/frame paths; shortcut summaries miss later revisions. Predictors do well on many supposedly rich cases. Initial-state models accurately predict outcomes across rich open trajectories, including stage/type paths. Trajectory prefixes, preregistered predictions, final outcomes, task complexity measures. Narrow P5 to specific classes of open-ended inquiry or replace with predictability taxonomy. Conjecture; no predictive test yet.
Type I: context-window disagreement is data-resolvable in principle. Equalize access to missing retained records while holding frame/comparison relation stable. Disagreement drops after sharing relevant records. Sharing records does not change outputs despite confirmed relevance. Independently confirmed Type I cases persist after full context equalization with no residual frame/provenance issue. Pre/post contexts, retained records, relevance rationale, output comparison. Type I needs subtypes for integration cost, trust, or frame dependence. Supported only by framework-self coding example.
Type II: frame disagreement persists after relevant data is shared. Equalize data, then test translation/adoption/third-frame interventions. Data-only moves fail; frame work changes outputs. Additional raw data often closes alleged Type II cases. Confirmed Type II cases reliably close by data sharing alone. Shared data audit, frame descriptions, intervention sequence, output changes. Redefine Type II or merge with Type I under practical conditions. Open.
Type III: absent-record disagreement cannot be resolved from R. Freeze record, test whether compatible event histories differ on target proposition. More search over R cannot decide; new external record or convention is required. Boundary of R or target proposition is hard to specify. Alleged absent-record facts repeatedly turn out decidable from retained traces. Record boundary, missingness proof or audit, compatible-history analysis. Make Type III a provisional status rather than positive type in most cases. Open; no external Type III case executed.
Formation stage: first emission of a statement is identifiable. Prospectively mark statement creation and compare coders. Coders agree on first emitted claim and later rounds refer to it. Multiple seed claims or imported history make first formation ambiguous. Coders cannot identify formation reliably even in controlled new bundles. Prospective round records, statement identity rules, coder agreement. Add pre-formation/imported-history stage or stronger identity rules. Works in framework-self by construction.
Enrichment stage: non-rival information addition is distinguishable. Code evidence/question rounds for live counter-position absence. Independent coders separate neutral additions from contestation. Covert advocacy and selective evidence often make enrichment labels unstable. Enrichment cannot be distinguished from advocacy/contestation above chance. Round text, party commitments, live-position set, coder rationales. Add "advocacy" or mixed-stage labels. Unvalidated.
Critique stage: probing without rival position is distinguishable. Code objection/question rounds for maintained counter-position. Coders agree when a probe tests assumptions without asserting rival claim. Critique often later becomes contestation, making retrospective labels outcome-dependent. Critique and contestation cannot be reliably separated from records. Round records, party commitments, follow-up positions, independent labels. Collapse critique into contestation or require uncertainty labels. Unvalidated beyond self-bundle.
Contestation stage: live counter-position can be identified. Prospectively record live positions and compare stage labels. Contestation labels align with explicit rival commitments. Implicit, strategic, or power-constrained positions are missed. Live disagreement cannot be identified reliably from records. Position registry, objections, claims, follow-up behavior, coder agreement. Type taxonomy becomes unusable except in formalized disputes. One retrospective framework-self contestation only.
Consolidation stage: settlement/current status can be recorded without overstating truth. Track consolidated claims and later supersessions/reopenings. Consolidations cite verdict/convention/underdetermination and later supersessions preserve history. Readers treat consolidation as truth or finality. Consolidation labels routinely hide unresolved disagreement or block correction. Consolidation rationales, dissent records, supersession history, reader/use audits. Rename to "current convention/status" or require dissent fields. Open; self-bundle remains open.
Explicit recording reduces re-litigation. Compare matched documented and undocumented inquiries with stable issue ids. Bundle-disciplined cases reopen settled issues less often without new evidence/frame rationale. Re-litigation is equally low in informal records or issue coding is subjective. Bundle cases re-litigate as much or more than matched undocumented inquiries. Matched trajectories, issue ids, consolidation markers, reopenings, task controls. Treat recording as provenance benefit only, not convergence benefit. Not measurable in self-bundle: no stable issue ids.

9. Design Consequences

The limitations above should affect future design, not sit as disclaimers.

  • Stage/type labels need rationales, uncertainty markers, and mixed-move escape hatches.
  • Type III should often be recorded as provisional underdetermination unless the record boundary is audited.
  • Metrics must publish representation choices and allow rival encodings.
  • Re-litigation claims require stable issue identifiers before they can be tested.
  • External cases must be coded prospectively where possible; retrospective classification should be marked as exploratory.
  • Behavioral and power variables should be recorded when materially relevant rather than hidden inside f.
  • The framework should remain open until external validation and rival-model comparisons show that it explains or improves something beyond its own repository discipline.
