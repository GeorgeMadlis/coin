A Computational Framework for Statement Evolution — v3

A Computational Framework for Statement Evolution — v3

Foundation: Wolfram observer theory (as formal analogy) · Extensions: computational vs. factual irreducibility; sequential inquiry chains and self-disagreement; stage-classified statement trajectories (formation, enrichment, critique, contestation, consolidation) · Delivery: OKF v0.1 fact-checking bundles with round records

Supersedes: v2 (framework_v2.md). Changes in v3: §1 retitled and reframed around the general statement trajectory, of which observer disagreement (multi-party or diagonal/self) is one regime — contestation — among five life-cycle stages; §2.2 gains a stage classification (formation, enrichment, critique, contestation, consolidation) layered over fc-move, restricts the Type I/II/III taxonomy and P1–P5 to contestation-stage rounds, generalizes v2's type trajectory to a stage trajectory (of which the type trajectory is the contestation restriction), formally realizes the sequential multi-observer regime as interleaved parties on one round sequence (parallel branches are not part of the operational model), and re-examines P4 as a stage-ordering conjecture (P4′); §3 gains the round-record fc-stage key (§3.4) and a new "Canonical state and derived artifacts" subsection (§3.7); Steps 0, 2, 3, 4, 6 updated accordingly; Steps 1, 5, 7, 8, 9 noted as extended by the stage layer without rewriting their prompts. framework_v2.md and all rounds/ledger lines recorded under it are unchanged and remain the historical record of the prior scope.

1. Purpose and assessment

Problem. A statement — a claim, an interpretation, a verdict — is rarely settled by a single emission. It is created, then added to, questioned, sometimes contested, and eventually consolidated or declared permanently open. The general object of the framework is therefore not a disagreement but a statement trajectory: the sequence of recorded rounds through which one statement evolves, from its first emission through however many rounds of enrichment, critique, contestation, and consolidation it accrues before the record stops growing (which may be never). A static disagreement between two parties is a single cross-section of one possible stage — contestation — of that trajectory; it is not the trajectory itself, and not every trajectory ever passes through it.

Disagreement — one party's interpretation diverging from another's, or from its own earlier interpretation — is the framework's original and still central case, and keeps its own name: observer disagreement names the contestation regime, i.e., the sub-sequence of rounds in which a live counter-position stands against the current claim. But a statement can equally evolve with no live disagreement at any point: someone adds corroborating evidence or answers a gap in the record (enrichment), someone probes an assumption or asks a frame-testing question without holding a rival position (critique), and the statement is eventually judged settled (consolidation) — three stages a trajectory can pass through, revisit, or skip entirely, without ever entering contestation. §2.2 formalizes this as a stage classification layered over every round, of which the three-type disagreement taxonomy carried over from v1/v2 becomes the classification of contestation-stage rounds specifically.

The framework must cover two regimes of contestation with the same machinery, carried over unchanged from v2:

  • Multi-party: two or more observers exchanging claims, objections, and evidence over rounds.
  • Single-party: one observer that keeps asking questions and adding evidence — digging deeper and deeper into a problem — while maintaining the records of the process. Self-disagreement (revising one's own earlier interpretation) is treated identically to disagreement with another party.

Both regimes are, per §2.2, realized operationally as one interleaved round sequence, never as separately-clocked parallel branches later merged: this project's own practice — several agents contributing sequentially to the same bundle, one PR at a time — is the concrete instance (see docs/agent-guide.md's Sequential Multi-Observer Rule, and its retirement of an earlier dual-agent fusion protocol that did use parallel branches per agent). Stage, like the multi-observer regime, is a property of the single shared trajectory, not of any one party's private state.

The goal is a theoretical framework for analysing such trajectories, and a practical delivery format (per fact-checking or knowledge-building problem) that records the trajectory itself, so that any human or AI agent can consume the current state — and its full derivation history — at a complexity level matched to their question.

Assessment: positive, with two qualifications.

Wolfram's observer theory supplies a formal analogy for something usually handled vaguely. In Wolfram's primary sources, observers are computationally bounded systems that equivalence many detailed states into reduced representations; useful observer narratives depend on pockets or slices of computational reducibility within generally computationally irreducible processes; the ruliad/rulial space supplies the space of computational possibility; observers sample it from bounded positions; and persistence through time is an explicit observer assumption. These claims are sourced to A New Kind of Science chapter 12 section 6, "Exploring Rulial Space," "The Concept of the Ruliad," "Twenty Years Later," "Computational Foundations for the Second Law of Thermodynamics," "Observer Theory," and "On the Nature of Time"; independent context is supplied by Rickles, Elshatlawy, Arsiwalla, and Blum's ruliology/observer-theory papers. This project's translation is narrower: two people who witnessed the same events but disagree are not necessarily disagreeing about facts. They may be applying different coarse-grainings to the surviving record: a limited context window C_i or a different framing/compression function f_i. The analogy to separation in rulial space is a project translation, not a measured physical distance. Nothing in this account is specific to disagreement: the same coarse-graining machinery describes an observer adding to its own context window (enrichment) or revising its compression function under a probing question (critique) with no rival observer in the picture at all.

The sequential motivation is also Wolfram-sourced but not imported literally: Wolfram's time essays describe time as progressive computational rewriting, with multiway histories and observer-bounded sampling. This project's translation is a statement trajectory: a path through interpretive states in which the outcome of a genuine inquiry often cannot be known without running the rounds. P5's "dialogic irreducibility" is therefore a project extension inspired by computational irreducibility, not a theorem stated by Wolfram.

Qualification 1 — analogy, not physics. Wolfram's physics program is speculative and not peer-established. The framework uses selected computational structures as a formal analogy — not as a claim that human disagreement, or statement evolution generally, literally is a physical process in the Wolfram model or has a measured rulial distance. The framework does not depend on the empirical truth of Wolfram's fundamental-physics program. Wolfram's writings are primary sources for what Wolfram proposes, not independent validation of those proposals.

Qualification 2 — must engage existing formal results. Aumann's agreement theorem (Bayesian observers with common priors and common knowledge cannot agree to disagree — so persistent disagreement already implies different priors or partitions); Simon's bounded rationality; algorithmic information theory (compression as understanding); coarse-graining in statistical mechanics; second-order cybernetics (von Foerster, Luhmann). The sequential regime additionally connects to Bayesian belief revision and the AGM belief-revision literature, Peirce's model of inquiry, and argumentation theory (Dung frameworks, dialogue games) — the framework's contribution relative to these is the computational-boundedness dimension and the explicit recording function. The factual-irreducibility extension (§2) further connects to Quine–Duhem underdetermination, analytic historiography (Danto, Ankersmit), and the reproducibility/provenance literature (W3C PROV, FAIR). The non-contestation stages (enrichment, critique, consolidation) connect most directly to Peirce's inquiry model and to the non-adversarial move types of argumentation theory's dialogue games; positioning this precisely is Step 1's task (see §4).

2. Formal core

2.1 The two-stage observer model (static cross-section)

A single-stage model (observer compresses the event stream directly) is insufficient, because observers of historical events never see the event stream. The corrected picture is a chain:

E  ──r──▶  R  ──fᵢ──▶  interpretationᵢ
  • E — the event stream (what actually happened).
  • r — the recording function: a lossy compression of E performed by past observers (witnesses, archivists, instruments, publication pipelines), with their own context windows and compression schemes. r is non-invertible, and its specification is itself often lost.
  • R = r(E) — the surviving record. The only thing any present observer can access.
  • Oᵢ = (Cᵢ, fᵢ) — a present observer: a context window Cᵢ ⊆ R and a computationally bounded compression function fᵢ inducing an equivalence relation over R.
  • Interpretation — fᵢ(R ∩ Cᵢ). Disagreement — f₁(R ∩ C₁) ≠ f₂(R ∩ C₂).

Attribution note: bounded observers, equivalencing/coarse-graining, pockets of reducibility, computational irreducibility, ruliad/rulial space, observer sampling, multiway histories, and observer persistence are direct Wolfram-source concepts. The variables O_i = (C_i, f_i), C_i, f_i, and f_i(R intersect C_i) are this project's formal translation. The recording function r, surviving record R = r(E), factual irreducibility, Type I/II/III taxonomy, stage taxonomy, and OKF provenance discipline are project extensions.

2.2 The dynamic model: observer trajectories, rounds, and stages

An observer is now indexed by time: O(t) = (C(t), f(t)) — an observer trajectory. A statement trajectory proceeds in rounds t = 0, 1, 2, …. A round consists of:

  1. an emitted claim — interpretation(t) = f(t)(R(t) ∩ C(t));
  2. a move against it: an objection, a question, new evidence e(t), a frame-change, a convention, or a verdict-revision;
  3. a state update: C(t+1) = C(t) ∪ e(t) (context accumulates) and/or f(t+1) = update(f(t)) (frame revision);
  4. a possibly revised claim, interpretation(t+1);
  5. (new in v3) a stage classification, stage(t), locating the round in the statement's life cycle (below) — layered over the move, not a substitute for it.

Two structural facts make the trajectory recursive rather than merely iterative:

  • The trajectory extends E. Every claim, objection, and evidence submission is itself a new event. The exchange appends to the event stream it is about.
  • The trajectory passes through r. Each round is recorded (or not). If the rounds are recorded lossily — or not at all — the trajectory's own history becomes a candidate for future Type III disputes ("what did we actually establish in round 12, and why?"). This is the theoretical motivation for the bundle's round records in §3: the bundle is the explicit recording function for the trajectory itself, guaranteeing that a trajectory maintained under the bundle discipline can never generate factual irreducibility about its own history.

Stage classification. Layered over every round's fc-move is a stage, one of five, mutually exclusive per round:

  • formation — the round that first emits the statement on this trajectory: creation moves, round 0 of that statement's history.
  • enrichment — addition of information with no standing counter-position: new evidence offered in support or elaboration, or a clarifying question that fills a gap in C(t).
  • critique — critical analysis by any party, including the trajectory's own past self, that does not assert or maintain a rival position: an objection raised as a probe, or a question that interrogates f(t) itself rather than merely C(t).
  • contestation — a live disagreement between positions: a standing counter-position stands against the current claim. This is the only stage at which the three-type taxonomy of §2.4 and propositions P1–P5 apply.
  • consolidation — convention adoption, verdict revision, supersession of a settled point, or declaration of underdetermined/bottomed-out status.

The five stages are not ordered a priori across a trajectory: a statement may cycle through enrichment and critique repeatedly, enter and leave contestation more than once, and later revisit an already-consolidated point (consolidation is supersedable, like any other bundle content — §3.5 rule 4). Only formation is fixed in position: it is definitionally the trajectory's first round for that statement (formalized as P4′(a), §2.5).

Stage ← fc-move. fc-stage is layered over fc-move, not derived from it by a fixed function: the same move type realizes different stages depending on whether a live counter-position exists at the time of the round.

fc-move possible stage(s) disambiguator
evidence formation (round 0 of the statement) · enrichment (later addition, no rival position) · contestation (a Type I move offered against a standing counter-position) Is this the statement's first round? Does a rival position currently stand?
question enrichment (fills a gap in C(t)) · critique (interrogates f(t) itself, no rival frame asserted) Does the question target missing context, or the frame itself?
objection critique (a probe, not a maintained rival claim) · contestation (the objection is, or provokes, a standing rival position) Does the objecting party hold and maintain a competing position after the round?
frame-change contestation (a party unilaterally adopts a diverging f, opening or extending disagreement) · consolidation (a frame is jointly adopted, closing a Type II gap) Is the new frame contested, or jointly adopted as settlement?
convention consolidation — (single mapping)
verdict-revision consolidation — (single mapping)

Type I/II/III and the type trajectory are properties of contestation rounds only (formalized in §2.4). Consequently the bundle-level record generalizes from v2's type trajectory to a stage trajectory — the sequence stage(0), stage(1), stage(2), … — of which the type trajectory (fc-type-at-round, defined only where stage(t) = contestation) is a sub-sequence, not the whole record.

Sequential multi-observer realization. A multi-observer round sequence is a single sequence of rounds t = 0, 1, 2, …, together with a party-assignment function p: ℕ → P (P the set of parties/agents — already recorded per round as fc-party), such that round t's move is authored by observer O_{p(t)}. All state lives on the one shared trajectory: C(t+1) = C(t) ∪ e(t) and f(t+1) = update(f(t)) regardless of which party made move t. Each observer O_a is therefore not a separately-clocked trajectory O_a(t) evolving on its own branch, but the restriction of the single shared trajectory to the rounds it authored, {t : p(t) = a}: agent a's every move acts on the full accumulated state left by all prior parties, never on a private fork later needing reconciliation. This is the formal content of "different agents as different subjects": interleaving realizes multi-observer disagreement without parallel branching. A parallel-branch realization — independent O_a(t), O_b(t) advancing on separate branches and later fused — is representable in the general model but is not how this project operationally realizes the multi-party regime; docs/agent-guide.md's retirement of the earlier dual-agent fusion protocol in favor of one branch per PR, agents contributing sequentially, is exactly the choice of the interleaved realization over the parallel-branch one.

The single-party diagonal case. When one party keeps asking questions and adding evidence, the "two observers" of the static model become time slices of one trajectory: contestation-stage disagreement is between O(t+1) and O(t) — the observer's present state versus its own earlier interpretation. Nothing else changes: self-disagreement classifies under exactly the same taxonomy. "I found new data" is Type I against one's past self; "I now frame it differently" is Type II; "I have hit ground that no further digging can recover" is Type III. Multi-party disagreement is the general case; solitary deep-digging is its diagonal — and the diagonal is not specific to contestation: a party can equally enrich or critique its own earlier round (self-enrichment, self-critique), with stage assigned exactly as in the table above.

2.3 Two kinds of irreducibility

Computational irreducibility Factual irreducibility
Nature of the limit Resource-relative Absolute (informational)
Where the information is All present, but extraction cannot be shortcut Absent from R; lost at recording time
Source relation Direct Wolfram concept: computational irreducibility interacting with computationally bounded observers; Second Law writings use the analogy of initial conditions becoming inaccessible to bounded observers Project extension: the past reaches the present only through non-invertible r
Recoverable by unbounded compute? Yes, in principle No, by any observer
Typical behaviour of disputes Shrink as resources grow Permanent at any resource level
Manifestation in a trajectory Rounds are slow/costly but productive Rounds accumulate without resolution

Dialogic irreducibility is this project's application of the computational kind to the trajectory itself: a statement trajectory is treated as a computation whose outcome cannot in general be shortcut — one must actually run the rounds (see P5). This is not stated by Wolfram as a human-inquiry theorem.

2.4 Three-type disagreement taxonomy

This taxonomy classifies contestation-stage rounds (§2.2); it does not apply to formation, enrichment, critique, or consolidation rounds, which have no live counter-position to classify.

  • Type I — different context windows (C₁ ≠ C₂ over the accessible record; or C(t+1) ⊋ C(t) in the diagonal case): data-resolvable by sharing. Even here, resolution is blocked in practice when unpacking someone else's compressed narrative back to raw events — to re-compress it one's own way — is computationally intractable for a bounded observer.
  • Type II — different compression functions (f₁ ≠ f₂; or f(t+1) ≠ f(t)): frame-resolvable only — by translation between frames or a jointly adopted third coarse-graining. No amount of additional shared data closes the gap, because the divergence is in the compression function, not the input.
  • Type III — disputed content lies outside R (factual irreducibility): not resolvable by data or by frames. At best convention-resolvable (the parties — or the digging party and its future self — agree on a shared inference procedure over surviving traces) or honestly declared permanently underdetermined.

Type descent. Within a contestation stretch, the classification is not fixed: it typically descends. Early rounds resolve Type I components (evidence is shared or acquired), which exposes the underlying Type II components (frames now confront each other directly), whose resolution or explicit conventionalization exposes the Type III residue — the part of the question the surviving record simply underdetermines. Digging deeper is a descent through the taxonomy, and it has a floor (P4, §2.5). Whether an analogous descent claim holds across stages — not just within one contestation stretch — is examined separately as P4′.

2.5 Key propositions

  • P1. Type II convergence is blocked in practice by the computational irreducibility of frame translation.
  • P2. Type III disagreements are invariant under both context enlargement and frame translation — a consequence of the non-invertibility of r.
  • P3. When the specification of r is itself lost, observers cannot even bound the underdetermination; Type III disputes are then undetectable from within and are systematically misclassified as Type II. This explains why parties to a historically underdetermined dispute each believe the other is merely biased — absent facts are silently filled by each observer's compression scheme.
  • P4 — type descent and termination (within a contestation stretch). A contestation whose moves are truthful and recorded descends the taxonomy monotonically in the limit: Type I components resolve first, then Type II components resolve or are conventionalized, and the stretch bottoms out at the Type III residue. Termination criterion: further rounds add records but no resolution — the remaining disagreement is invariant under both new evidence and frame moves. Reaching this floor is a result, and the bundle should state it as such (fc-status: underdetermined), not disguise it as an open question.
  • P4′ — stage ordering (conjecture, not theorem; new in v3). Does type descent generalize to a claim about the ordering of stages across a trajectory? Two parts, with different status:
  • (a) Formation precedes — provable, near-definitional. Formation (round 0 of a given statement) precedes every enrichment, critique, contestation, or consolidation round about that statement, since no round can update a claim that has not yet been emitted.
  • (b) Stages trend toward consolidation — only plausible, not provable. This would be the substantive stage-level analogue of type descent, and it does not hold as a monotone claim across a whole trajectory: enrichment and critique are not linearly ordered relative to each other and may recur any number of times, and contestation can be entered, resolved, and re-entered, because a consolidated point can itself be superseded (§3.5 rule 4), reopening critique or contestation on the same statement. Unlike type descent within one contestation stretch — a claim about a bounded, terminating sub-process (P4) — there is no comparable floor for the stage sequence as a whole: a trajectory does not "bottom out" at consolidation the way a contestation bottoms out at Type III, because consolidation is not a terminal sink. Per Standing Caveat 3, P4′(b) is asserted only as a conjecture: at most it is plausible that (i) once contestation is entered, P4's descent still holds unchanged within it, and (ii) a critique round preceding a statement's first contestation round correlates with a shorter subsequent contestation stretch and a lower post-consolidation supersession rate — both restated as a testable prediction in §4 Step 6.
  • P5 — dialogic irreducibility. The outcome of a genuine statement trajectory cannot in general be predicted without running it. Corollaries: (a) a party cannot legitimately pre-announce where the trajectory will converge; (b) shortcutting rounds (skipping the descent) does not accelerate convergence but misclassifies the disagreement type, typically mistaking Type II or III for Type I.

2.6 The climate-model case is a hybrid — and diagnosing it is a contribution

When a published climate-model prediction cannot be reproduced, two distinct causes are usually conflated:

  1. Computational irreducibility: code, exact version, forcing datasets, initial conditions, and tuning choices all survive, but re-running is prohibitively expensive. Recoverable in principle, bounded in practice.
  2. Factual irreducibility: any element of the provenance is lost — undocumented tuning, a silently revised forcing dataset, an unarchived code version. The computation itself has become a historical event with a lossy record.

The framework predicts these populations behave differently (prediction 4, §4 Step 6): the first yields disagreements that shrink as compute gets cheaper; the second yields disagreements that are permanent regardless of resources. In trajectory terms: reproduction attempts on the first population are productive contestation or enrichment rounds; on the second they accumulate without resolution — a P4 floor.

2.7 The EUDR system as demonstrated countermeasure

Deterministic pipelines, SHA256 manifests, vendored WSDL/XSD snapshots, dated evidence bundles under evidence/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<run_id>/ — this is engineering that prevents present verdicts from becoming Type III disputes in the future. An evidence bundle is a recording function r made explicit, complete, and invertible with respect to the verdict: any future observer can re-derive f(R) exactly. Likewise, Hansen GFC vs. JRC GFC2020 are two observers of the same physical forest with different context windows (annual loss-year band vs. baseline-only semantics) and different coarse-grainings (canopy thresholds, pixel geometry); the asymmetric verdict rule (one credible loss signal suffices to FAIL; one zero-loss signal never suffices to PASS) is a meta-observer aggregation rule — a Type II disagreement resolved by frame-level convention rather than data-sharing. The iterative refinement of the EUDR documentation itself — successive sessions adding evidence, correcting framings, superseding earlier claims — is a live single-party statement trajectory usable as a case study (§4 Step 7d), one that visibly passes through enrichment and critique rounds as well as contestation.

3. Delivery format: OKF fact-checking bundles with round records

3.1 Why OKF fits the theory

The Open Knowledge Format (OKF v0.1, published by Google Cloud, 12 June 2026) represents knowledge as a directory of markdown files with YAML frontmatter, authored and consumed by both humans and AI agents. Relevant constraints: a bundle is a self-contained hierarchical collection; each concept is one markdown document whose file path (minus .md) is its identity; links are ordinary markdown links whose relationship kind is conveyed by surrounding prose; index.md files support progressive disclosure; log.md records chronological change history; consumers must tolerate broken links; the only required frontmatter field is type (recommended: title, description, resource, tags, timestamp); producers may add custom keys and consumers must tolerate unknown keys. OKF explicitly does not subsume domain-specific schemas — it references them — so evidence manifests, WSDLs, and code repos are linked resources, not content to be re-encoded.

The bundle closes the framework's loop by making four normally-implicit things explicit:

  1. R — pinned by links to code at exact commits, input data with checksums, evidence manifests → the Type III countermeasure for the subject matter.
  2. f — the method, documented in the S axis.
  3. A graded family of coarse-grainings f₀ ⊃ f₁ ⊃ f₂ ⊃ f₃ — the same content at increasing resolution; the layman answer is the maximal compression, the deepest level approaches the raw record. Progressive disclosure = an observer choosing how far down the reducibility hierarchy to descend.
  4. The statement trajectory itself (inquiry chain in v2) — every round recorded, every revision a supersession, never a silent edit → the Type III countermeasure for the trajectory's own history. log.md, peripheral in v1, is central from v2 on: it is the trajectory's ledger.

Diagnostic use. When two parties — or one party and its earlier self — dispute a claim, the layered structure localizes the disagreement: find the deepest common level at which the accounts still coincide, per round. Divergence at R-level with agreement on S → data dispute (Type I). Divergence at S with agreement on R-inputs → frame dispute (Type II). Divergence about content absent from the reproduction layer → Type III. The sequence of per-round classifications, restricted to contestation-stage rounds (§2.2), is the trajectory's type trajectory; under P4 it should descend within a contestation stretch, and a stretch stuck at one type is itself diagnostic (a Type I that never resolves despite data-sharing is probably a misclassified Type II or III — cf. P3).

3.5 Design rules (theory → format)

  1. Every level is self-contained for its reader. L0 is not a teaser but a complete answer at maximal compression. Deeper levels add resolution, never contradict shallower ones. (Revised in v2:) the consistency condition applies to the current version of the bundle. When deeper digging does uncover a contradiction with a shallower claim — the normal and expected outcome of a productive round — the shallower claim is corrected as a recorded round: the old text is superseded (fc-supersedes, git history), the round file states what changed and why, and log.md ledgers it. Never a silent edit.
  2. The reproduction axis is mandatory and pinned. code.md references an exact commit; data.md exact dataset versions with checksums. An unpinned link is a future Type III dispute.
  3. Declare irreducibility explicitly. Where a result cannot be cheaply re-derived, state whether the obstacle is computational (re-run expensive but fully specified) or factual (some input no longer recoverable — mark it, consistent with the existing UNVERIFIED inline convention). Where a contestation stretch has bottomed out (P4), declare fc-status: underdetermined rather than leaving the question artificially open.
  4. Append-only supersession. (New in v2.) The bundle grows monotonically at the provenance level: rounds are never deleted or renumbered; revisions supersede rather than overwrite; the current state is always derivable from round 0 plus the ledger. This is what makes consolidation non-terminal (P4′(b)): a consolidated concept file can itself be superseded by a later round, reopening critique or contestation on the same statement without erasing the earlier settlement's record. This rule is what makes the bundle the explicit recording function for the trajectory itself — a single party can dig arbitrarily deep over months of sessions and never lose the ability to answer "what did I believe at round t, and what moved me off it?"

3.6 Agent contract: proxy observer and participating observer

Consumption (proxy observer) — unchanged from v1: read root index.md → classify the user's question by implied level and axis ("So is it compliant?" → L0; "Why 10% canopy and not 30%?" → S/L2–L3; "Can I re-run this?" → REPRO; "How did we get here?" / "What changed since last time?" → INQUIRY) → descend only as deep as the question requires → answer from that level, citing the concept file path → offer the next level down as follow-up. Two hard rules: never answer at a deeper level than asked (imposing the agent's own coarse-graining); never at a shallower level than the question requires (lossy beyond the questioner's request).

Participation (participating observer) — new in v2. When the user's message is a move (an objection, new evidence, a follow-up question that extends the trajectory) rather than a pure query, the agent: (1) opens a new round file with the move recorded verbatim or pinned; (2) classifies the round's stage (and, if contestation, the live disagreement type) and states the rationale; (3) applies resulting revisions under the supersession rule; (4) updates log.md and inquiry/index.md; (5) answers from the revised state. The agent's own contributions are moves like any other — logged, attributable, supersedable. This makes the single-party deep-digging regime concrete: the human asks, the agent answers and records, and the bundle accumulates the full trajectory across sessions with no dependence on either party's memory.

Write this contract into the bundle itself (agent-contract.md) so any OKF-consuming agent inherits both behaviours without bespoke integration.

3.7 Canonical state and derived artifacts

The committed HEAD of main is the canonical current state of every bundle: any question of the form "what does the record currently say" is answered by reading HEAD, not a branch, a working tree, or a cached export (this is docs/agent-guide.md's Canonical-State Rule, stated here as part of the theory it operationalizes). Everything else derived from a bundle — rendered HTML (e.g. the _site/ trees produced by okf-fc render, cf. bundles/README.md), exported zips, cross-bundle catalogs, and visualizations of the stage/type trajectory — is a derived, commit-pinned artifact: it must record the commit it was generated from, and is stale (not silently trusted as current) the moment HEAD moves past that commit.

Two round counters are in play. The root log.md records repository rounds (repo_round): every merged repository change, whether it affects bundles or only project-level infrastructure. A bundle's log.md and inquiry/round-NNNN.md files record bundle rounds (bundle_round): moves in that bundle's statement trajectory only. A repository round may therefore have no bundle round, one corresponding bundle round, or several corresponding bundle rounds across different bundles. Published snapshots are named by bundle round (<date>-r<bundle_round>-<shorthash>) and record source_repo_round separately in their manifest/catalog metadata; naming snapshots by repo round would make the zip contradict its internal bundle index whenever the counters diverge. §4 Step 4's generator/validator is the forward reference for tooling that produces and pins such artifacts; a validator run under this rule should check that a bundle's derived _site/ (or any future export) records its source commit and is regenerable byte-for-byte from it.

Step 2 — Formal core.

Formalize (a) the static two-stage chain E → r → R → observers Oᵢ = (Cᵢ, fᵢ) computing fᵢ(R ∩ Cᵢ), and (b) the dynamic model: observer trajectories O(t) = (C(t), f(t)); rounds as the operator (claim(t), move, e(t)) ↦ (C(t+1), f(t+1), claim(t+1)) with C(t+1) = C(t) ∪ e(t); the recursion whereby each round appends to E and passes through r; the diagonal (single-party) case as disagreement between time slices of one trajectory, generalized to any stage's diagonal; and, new in v3, (c) the stage classification function stage: rounds → {formation, enrichment, critique, contestation, consolidation}, the stage ← fc-move mapping (a move type may realize different stages depending on whether a standing counter-position exists), and the sequential multi-observer realization: a party-assignment function p: ℕ → P over one shared round sequence, with each observer O_a defined as the restriction {t : p(t) = a} of that single trajectory rather than an independently-clocked trajectory on its own branch — contrast explicitly with a parallel-branch realization and state why the bundle discipline in §3 adopts the former. Derive the three-type taxonomy as a classification of contestation-stage rounds only, and its dynamic reading (per-contestation-round classification, type trajectory as the contestation-restriction of the stage trajectory). Prove or argue: (P1) Type II convergence is blocked in practice by computational irreducibility of frame translation; (P2) Type III disagreements are invariant under both context enlargement and frame translation — prove from non-invertibility of r; (P3) when the specification of r is itself lost, Type III disputes are undetectable from within and systematically misclassified as Type II; (P4) truthful, recorded contestation stretches descend the taxonomy and bottom out at the Type III residue — state the termination criterion (invariance of the residual disagreement under both evidence and frame moves) and required assumptions (truthfulness, recording discipline, bounded frame set or a fairness condition on moves); (P4′) whether type descent generalizes to a claim about the ordering of stages across a whole trajectory — prove the near-definitional part (formation precedes all other stages for a given statement) and argue why the stronger part (stages trend toward consolidation) is not provable in general (consolidation is supersedable, so there is no stage-level floor analogous to the Type III residue) — state it as a conjecture per Standing Caveat 3, together with its two derived plausible sub-claims (descent still holds within any entered contestation stretch; a preceding critique round correlates with a shorter subsequent contestation stretch and lower post-consolidation supersession); (P5) dialogic irreducibility — the outcome of a genuine trajectory cannot in general be predicted without running it; derive the corollaries (no legitimate pre-announcement of convergence; shortcutting rounds misclassifies type). Assess for each proposition whether it is provable or merely plausible, and under what assumptions; P4's monotonicity in particular must be examined for counterexamples (frame oscillation, adversarial moves), and P4′(b) must be examined for the reopening-by-supersession counterexample already identified in §2.5.

Step 6 — Derived predictions.

From the formal core, derive testable predictions: (1) sharing raw data reduces Type I but not Type II disagreement; (2) disagreement persistence correlates with compression-scheme distance, not information asymmetry; (3) convergence requires either translation between frames or a jointly adopted third frame; (4) irreproducible scientific claims split into cost-limited and provenance-limited populations with different convergence behaviour under growing resources; (5) disagreements over thinly recorded events are more persistent and more frame-driven than over well-recorded events; (6) recorded contestation stretches exhibit type descent — the modal order of resolution is I before II before III — and violations of descent predict either untruthful moves or misclassification; (7) single-party chains maintained under bundle discipline show measurably lower re-litigation (repeat rounds over already-settled points) than undocumented inquiries of comparable depth; (8) (new in v3, testing P4′) statements whose trajectory includes at least one critique-stage round before their first contestation-stage round reach consolidation in fewer contestation rounds, and are superseded less often after consolidation, than statements that enter contestation directly from formation or enrichment. Write the limitations section honestly. 5. Standing caveats

  1. Analogy discipline. Every Wolfram-derived claim in the paper must be traceable to a computational definition that stands independently of the physics program.
  2. OKF is a draft. v0.1 is explicitly a starting point; declare the targeted version in the spec and keep custom keys namespaced (fc-) against future reserved-key collisions.
  3. P2/P3 remain load-bearing; P4/P5 join them, and P4′ (stage ordering) joins as a conjecture from the outset. P4's monotonicity (type descent within a contestation stretch) is the most exposed claim carried from v2 — frame oscillation and adversarial moves are the obvious counterexample families, and Step 2 must either restrict the assumptions until it holds or downgrade it to an empirical regularity tested at Step 6 prediction (6). P4′(b) — the claim that stages trend toward consolidation across a whole trajectory — is weaker still and is asserted only as a conjecture (§2.5): consolidation is supersedable, so no stage-level floor analogous to the Type III residue is claimed; only its two narrower sub-claims (descent survives within an entered contestation stretch; critique-before-contestation predicts a shorter stretch, tested at Step 6 prediction 8) are put forward as plausible. If P4, P5, or P4′ turn out to be merely plausible rather than provable under stated assumptions, soften from "theorem" to "conjecture with supporting argument" before anything downstream is built.
  4. Round discipline has a cost. Full round records are justified for contested or long-running problems; the spec should define a lightweight profile (ledger-only, no per-round files) for low-stakes bundles, with an upgrade path.
