title
The problem in plain terms
type
Finding

The project asks how a statement — about events, a method, or a result — evolves as people, institutions, or AI agents keep working on it: added to, questioned, sometimes disputed, sometimes settled. It names this a statement trajectory and treats disagreement as the best-understood and most consequential stage of that trajectory, not the whole of it.

Disagreement — why people, institutions, or AI agents can refer to the same events and still not agree about what those events mean — remains a core question. The ordinary causes are different information and different framing. One observer may have seen records the other has not, or the two may sort the same records through different categories, priorities, and narratives. The framework adds a third cause: some information may no longer be recoverable. Historical events reach the present through records, and records are always selective. If a needed detail was never recorded, lost, or recorded without enough provenance, no amount of later argument can fully restore it.

But a statement's trajectory need not pass through live disagreement at all. Someone can add corroborating evidence without anyone contesting anything (enrichment). Someone can probe an assumption or ask a hard question without holding a rival position (critique). And a statement can be formally settled — a verdict adopted, a convention agreed, or a point declared permanently unrecoverable (consolidation) — with or without a contested stretch in between. Disagreement proper, named the contestation stage, is what happens when a standing rival position is on the table; the other stages are how a statement grows and gets tested without one.

This also covers one party questioning itself over time, in any of the stages above. A later version of a person or agent can disagree with an earlier version because it found new evidence, changed framing, or reached the floor where the surviving record cannot answer the question — or it can simply add to, or critique, its own earlier round without disagreeing with it at all.
